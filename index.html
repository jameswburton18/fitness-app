The week
Why this plan

Built around the WHO / ACSM guidelines: strength work for all major muscle groups at least twice a week, plus 150+ minutes of moderate activity. You get three strength sessions, one conditioning session, and two mobility days — each 25–35 minutes, no equipment.

Every session quietly doubles as rehab:

Big toe — turf-toe-style strains recover with gradual loading, not rest alone. Toe mobility and short-foot drills live in every lower-body warm-up, and the calf-raise ladder progresses you from slow raises to single-leg, to pogo hops, to strides — the on-ramp back to pain-free sprinting.

Knees — wall sits helping you is textbook: isometrics calm tendon pain. The knee ladder starts there and adds load gradually (tempo squats → split squats → step-downs → rear-foot-elevated) so the joint adapts instead of flaring.

Lower back — the core block is the McGill Big 3 (curl-up, side plank, bird dog): the best-evidenced trio for building spare capacity after a disc injury, training the spine to stay stiff while the hips move. Hinge patterning on hamstring day teaches the same thing under movement.

Hamstring — aching hamstrings respond to eccentric strength. Bridges progress to long-lever and slider curls — the home version of the Nordic curl, the single best-evidenced hamstring exercise.

Pain traffic light

Use this during any exercise, any day:

0–3 out of 10, settles by the next morning — carry on. Mild discomfort during rehab loading is normal and fine.

4–5, or worse the next morning — drop one level on that ladder, or halve the range of motion. Don't skip it, shrink it.

6+, sharp, or pain that changes how you move — stop that exercise today. Come back a level lower next session.

When to level up

Finish all sets at the top of the rep or time range, feeling like you had 2–3 more reps in the tank, on two sessions in a row → take the next rung. The app asks you after each workout; be honest in both directions.

Return to sprinting

The toe ladder is the gate: only move to pogo hops once single-leg calf raises are pain-free, and only try strides (70–80% runs) once 3×20 pogo hops are comfortable. First sprint session: 4–6 × 50m building to 90%, on a good day, fully warmed up.

Equipment, honestly

Nothing is required. Two cheap things would upgrade this a lot if you ever feel like it: a doorway pull-up bar (~£15, fixes the one real gap — pulling strength) and a long loop resistance band (~£10, adds rows and Spanish squats for your knees). No faff, no adjusting. Skip the dumbbells.
