Why this plan

Left knee

It started in August after a slide at tennis, and it’s getting better. The X-ray shows mild wear and tear (early osteoarthritis): slightly thicker bone under the cartilage and a small bony spur. That’s very common, and on its own it often doesn’t match how much a knee hurts.

The pain pattern, worse going down stairs, in low squats and after long sitting, and better with a bath and moving about, fits the kneecap joint being irritated. For both, the main treatment is the same: stronger thigh and hip muscles, built up gradually.

Right knee

A knock on hard furniture, with pain at the top of the knee. The same strength work helps, and it should settle with time. If it hasn’t eased in 6–8 weeks, get it checked.

Going downstairs

Going up is fine; coming down hurts. Coming down asks the thigh to control the knee as it bends under your weight. So the knee ladder ends with slow step-downs, which are that exact movement, made a little harder each week.

For the next few weeks
  • Skip kneeling and very deep squats outside the app. Depth comes back as the knee ladder climbs.
  • On stairs, lead down with the stronger leg and use the rail while it’s sore.
  • Walking is great. Keep doing your usual distance.
  • Judge progress by the morning checks and the fortnightly benchmarks, not day to day.
  • See your GP or a physio if a knee swells up, locks, gives way, turns hot and red, your calf becomes painful or swollen, or it’s getting worse rather than better.
Pain traffic light

Use this during any exercise, any day:

0–3 out of 10, settled by the next morning: carry on, and level up when it’s easy. Mild discomfort during rehab loading is normal.

4–5, or not settled by the next morning: stay on that rung or drop one, or halve the range. Don’t skip it, shrink it.

6+, sharp, or pain that changes how you move: stop that exercise today and come back a rung lower next session.

When to level up

Finish all sets at the top of the range, feeling like you had 2–3 more reps in the tank and within the green light, two sessions in a row. Then take the next rung. The app asks after each workout. Be honest in both directions.

The knee is stricter. Knees react the next day, so it also needs the next morning to be back to normal: “Easy” plus 2 settled mornings in a row (3 in stage 1). Sore the next morning drops it a rung.

Back to running and tennis

Running puts several times your bodyweight through the knee with every step, so it’s built up in steps:

  1. Knee ladder at the loaded step-down, step-downs ≤2/10 and a brisk 30-minute walk with no flare → pogo hops unlock in the Back to running ladder.
  2. 3 × 20s pogo hops and skipping, fine the next morning → walk-jog: 1 min easy jog / 2 min walk × 8, every other day. Add a little jogging each week while mornings stay settled.
  3. 20 minutes of easy continuous jogging → side-to-side steps and gentle tennis movement drills.
  4. Drills feel normal → a relaxed hit, then back to matches. A physio check before this step is a good idea.

These are sensible checkpoints, not guarantees. Mild arthritis isn’t a reason to stop running for good.
